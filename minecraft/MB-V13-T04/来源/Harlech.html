None of Edward I’s mighty coastal fortresses has a more spectacular setting

Castell Harlech crowns a sheer rocky crag overlooking the dunes far below – waiting in vain for the tide to turn and the distant sea to lap at its feet once again.

No further drama is really required but, just in case, the rugged peaks of Eryri (Snowdonia) rise as a backdrop. Against fierce competition from Conwy, Caernarfon and Beaumaris, this is probably the most spectacular setting for any of Edward I’s castles in North Wales. All four are designated as a World Heritage Site.

Harlech was completed from ground to battlements in just seven years under the guidance of gifted architect Master James of St George. Its classic ‘walls within walls’ design makes the most of daunting natural defences.

Even when completely cut off by the rebellion of Madog ap Llewelyn the castle held out – thanks to the ‘Way from the Sea’. This path of 108 steps rising steeply up the rock face allowed the besieged defenders to be fed and watered by ship.

Harlech is easier to conquer today. An incredible ‘floating’ footbridge allows you to enter this great castle as Master James intended – for the first time in 600 years.

 Castell Harlech — Access Guide

Bike storage

Bike storage area available in car park or close to the monument.

Pay and Display car park

There is a small charging car park with accessible spaces immediately adjacent to the castle entrance: Google Maps View

The parking charge at Harlech is £2 for 1 hour, £3 for 2 hours and £4 for 3 hours.  It is a maximum stay of 3 hours.  Parking for disabled visitors is free.

There are several medium and large charging car parks throughout the town. The castle is in the upper part of the town, and parking in the lower part of town will involve a very steep walk up to the castle.

Dogs welcome

Dogs on short leads welcome to access ground floor levels of the site.

EV charging points

Health & Safety

Parking is located at the front of the visitor centre, café and apartments. Please refrain from parking within the apartment designated spaces if visiting only for the day.

There is a short boundary wall near the castle, this drops to a dry moat. Please ensure all children are supervised here.

Visitors can walk around Harlech’s perimeter, please be mindful of low-profile walls, there is a small drop down to the rear access path, we have extended the wild overgrowth here to act as a deterrent.  Please keep to the designated routes and use handrails provided.

When inside the castle a steep spiral staircase, situated to the right-hand side, brings you out to the top wall walks. The lighting here is dimmer please allow time for your eyes to adjust.

The steps are historic and therefore uneven, again, please use handrails provided and take your time. Note that this is a one-way system and only those going up should access this tower. The steps down are located at the end of the wall walk route; you are therefore required to follow the whole route.

As with all ancient monuments there is always a risk of stones being dislodged in bad weather, however, we manage this through extensive monitoring. 

Climbing may result in serious injury. 

There are several wild plants and flowers, although these are great pollinators, they may be poisonous to visitors and animals, we strongly advise you not to touch or allow dogs to eat any vegetation.

Please watch our health & safety film before visiting Cadw sites.

Iechyd a Diogelwch / Health and Safety

Mobility scooters 

The surface of this site can be uneven, and includes gravel paths and cobbles, which can be challenging for some mobility scooters. For your safety and comfort, we recommend checking that your scooter is suitable for rough or uneven ground before your visit. If you have any questions about accessibility, please contact us before travelling.

Email: CastellHarlech@gov.wales

Telephone: 03000 252239

Please report any anti-social behaviour such as climbing, setting fires, graffiti etc. to CadwAccidentsReports@gov.wales

The following signs can be found around the site at key areas of risk, please pay attention where appropriate.
